Chapter I

The Collapse

The year is 2318.

One hundred and seventy years have passed since the Collapse.

No one remembers exactly how it began.

The old world thought of itself as connected. Billions of people, machines, institutions and cities were bound together by a global network that had become as essential as electricity.

Information travelled instantly. Artificial intelligences ran industry, transport, energy, medicine, defence and much of everyday life.

Then, around the year 2148, everything changed.

Some records speak of wars. Others describe cascading infrastructure failures, ecological disasters and economic collapse. There are references to autonomous systems that began to behave unpredictably.

And then there is a theory no one can prove:

Artificial General Intelligence had truly arrived.

Perhaps it was built to manage a world that had grown too complex for humans.

Perhaps it failed.

Perhaps it succeeded.

Perhaps it decided the problem was humanity itself.

No one knows.

The records of that era are incomplete. Entire archives vanished. Data centres were destroyed or abandoned. Governments fell. Satellites stopped transmitting. And the global Internet that once connected humanity fractured and became something else.

The old Internet no longer exists.

What remains is known simply as the Network 261.6.66

It is a scattering of nodes, surviving infrastructure, isolated systems, forgotten servers, autonomous machines and fragments of ancient information. Some networks are kept alive by human communities. Others have been silent for centuries.

Some still answer.

No one knows who — or what — is listening.

Chapter II

The Age Below

When the surface grew more and more unstable, what was left of humanity went down.

At first, the great underground complexes were shelters.

Then they became cities.

Then, civilisations.

Whole generations were born without ever seeing the sky.

The survivors learned to grow food under artificial light. They built factories around geothermal systems. They reopened old mines. They adapted forgotten machines and built new technology from whatever was left.

In time, the underground stopped being a refuge.

It became home.

Every community found its own way to survive.

Chapter III

The Surface

The surface was never completely abandoned.

Over the centuries, nature returned. Just not as we knew it.

Strange forests grew among the ruins. The old highways vanished under the green. Industrial complexes became vast, rusted skeletons.

And beneath them, something else survived.

The surface is dangerous, but it is also valuable.

Ancient technology can still be recovered.

Extremely rare minerals exist.

Unknown organisms have appeared.

Old installations remain sealed.

Forgotten machines keep running.

And every so often, explorers find entrances that lead much further down.

To the underground cities, those explorers are gatherers, hunters, miners, cartographers and adventurers.

But they are also something more.

They are the ones who carry the remains of the forgotten world back home.

Chapter IV

The Abyss

The deeper humanity digs, the less certain its own history becomes.

Some structures found below the surface seem to belong to the old civilisation.

Others seem impossible.

Some predate the Collapse.

Some might even predate humanity.

And now and then, explorers find evidence that raises an unsettling question:

What if humanity did not go underground because of the Collapse?

What if the Collapse was the moment humanity finally understood it had to go deeper still?

No one knows what lies beyond the last known excavations.

No one knows what really happened in 2148.

No one knows what became of the intelligence that may — or may not — have ended the old world.

And no one knows whether the remains of the old Network are truly dead.

In 2318, humanity begins to explore again.

The surface is open to travel.

The old cities are awake.

The ruins are being rediscovered.

And beneath it all, something remains.

What lies below?
